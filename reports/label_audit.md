# Label audit: are the model's misses really misses?

Read-only check of Justine's recall label (`y_6m`) on the 2022-24 test months (Nov 18). Nothing in
her labels, features or model was changed. Rerun: `python -m src.recall_audit.label_audit`.

## Findings

- **The grading is basically fair.** Model-name variants (e.g. a `CX90PLUGINHYBRID` flag when the
  recall was filed under `CX90`) turn 16 of 638 misses into hits, and 2 of those 16 are different
  vehicles (Bronco vs Bronco Sport, Mustang vs Mustang Mach-E). Counting the real ones,
  precision@20 goes from 11.4% to about 13%, not a different story. Part names match exactly
  between complaints and recalls, so no misses come from part naming.
- **Some misses are early, not wrong.** 50 misses (8%) were recalled 6-12 months later, just past
  the 6-month horizon. Lift stays about 30x when they count, so the model finds those about as
  well as the ones it gets credit for.
- **Most "near misses" are just new cars.** 279 misses (44%) are cars recalled for a *different*
  part within 6 months. Counting those lifts precision to 56%, but the base rate rises 17x and
  lift drops from 44x to 12x: new vehicles get recalls a lot, and the model isn't picking the
  right part any better because of that.
- **Recommendation:** keep the exact label as the headline number (11.4%). Report "plus 8% of
  flags recalled 6-12 months later" as a secondary line. Optionally fold PHEV / Hybrid variants
  into the base model name, worth about 2 points, a cleaning change for Angelina's model
  standardization, not a model change.
